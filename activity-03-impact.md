Who benefits, and roughly how many:
Directly benefits ~80–100 million urban and semi-urban Indian households (homemakers, working couples, elderly retirees living alone, and flatmates in PGs) who rely daily on 3 to 6 informal doorstep services. Secondarily benefits ~15–20 million micro-vendors and gig workers (milk delivery agents, 20L water-can suppliers, neighborhood presswalas/dhobis, and domestic workers) whose monthly income depends on timely, undisputed settlements.

What changes compared with today:
Today, families rely on kitchen wall calendars, scraps of paper, or mental math. Because unlocking a phone to type a ₹40 delivery in an expense app takes 40 seconds with full hands, manual apps get abandoned within a week. At month-end, households and vendors routinely clash over disputed counts ("18th ko do can nahi, ek hi can aaya tha"), causing awkward arguments, damaged relationships, or silent overpayments of ₹400–₹1,500 every month. With RozHisaab, logging takes a 3-second natural spoken sentence right as the door closes ("dedh litre doodh, 2 water can, 1 khali jar"). Real-time voice logging eliminates tracking fatigue, protects jar deposit balances, and replaces stressful 1st-of-the-month haggling with an agreed, transparent date-stamped record.

How you'd know it worked:
1. Habit Retention: Achieve ≥45% Day-30 active retention (households voice-logging deliveries at least 5 days a week), exceeding the typical <10% retention of manual typing ledgers.
2. Voice Parsing Accuracy: ≥92% first-pass accuracy in extracting multi-vendor items and regional fractions ("dedh", "paune do") without requiring manual text edits.
3. Settlement Velocity & Dispute Rate: ≥80% of vendor bills settled within the first 3 days of the month via one-click bilingual WhatsApp summaries, with reported billing disputes dropping by over 70% in active households.

The main risk:
Asymmetric vendor trust. The biggest risk is that while the household logs accurately by voice, the informal vendor still relies on their pocket diary or memory. If the vendor insists on their own number at month-end, a digital log that lives only on the customer’s phone can still trigger an argument. To mitigate this, RozHisaab offers an optional daily or weekly WhatsApp ping to the vendor ("Bhaiya, aaj ka hisaab note ho gaya"), establishing a two-way paper trail before month-end arrives.
